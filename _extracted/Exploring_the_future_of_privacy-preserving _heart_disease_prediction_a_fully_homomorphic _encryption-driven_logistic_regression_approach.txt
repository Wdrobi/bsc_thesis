Abstract 
Homomorphic Encryption (HE) offers a revolutionary cryptographic approach to safe-
guarding privacy in machine learning (ML), especially in processing sensitive healthcare 
data. This study aims to address the critical issue of privacy-preserving heart disease 
prediction by developing a novel Homomorphic Encryption-Driven Logistic Regression 
(HELR) framework, leveraging the Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song (CKKS) encryption scheme. 
The framework was implemented using the TenSeal and Torch libraries and evaluated 
on encrypted heart disease datasets with varying polynomial degrees. The study’s 
design involved applying the HELR model to three healthcare datasets and compar-
ing its performance with Support Vector Machines (SVM). The major findings revealed 
that the HELR model achieved high accuracy, within 1% to 3% of its non-HE counter-
part, while maintaining competitive computational efficiency. Furthermore, the HELR 
framework demonstrated robust security against various privacy attacks, including poi-
soning, evasion, member inference, model inversion, and model extraction, at dif-
ferent ML stages. Notably, the HELR model outperformed SVM in terms of accuracy, 
showcasing its effectiveness for secure healthcare predictions. The results suggest 
that HE-enhanced models can offer secure, accurate predictions, paving the way 
for advancements in privacy-preserving healthcare analytics. However, the study iden-
tified limitations related to the computational overhead introduced by HE and the scal-
ability of the model for large datasets. Future work will focus on optimizing encryption 
techniques and exploring parallel processing methods to address these challenges.
Keywords: CKKS, Gradient Descent, Healthcare, Heart disease prediction, 
Homomorphic Encryption, Logistic Regression
Introduction

address real-world challenges across diverse domains such as artificial intelligence, bio -
informatics, medical sciences, and marketing. ML can extract valuable insights from 
extensive datasets without requiring deep domain-specific expertise. However, the 
healthcare industry faces a significant hurdle in safeguarding patient privacy and sensi -
tive medical information.

Homomorphic Encryption (HE) (Salim (2022)) [16] has emerged as a compelling solu -
tion for addressing this concern. HE encryption technique permits specific mathematical 
operations to be executed on encrypted data without decryption. This capability enables 
computations to be performed directly on encrypted data, with the results remaining 
encrypted. This feature is particularly advantageous when sensitive data must be pro -
cessed in untrusted environments or by third parties, to ensure privacy and security.
Traditional privacy-preserving techniques in ML [17, 18, 26], such as anonymization, 
perturbation, randomization, and condensation, aim to protect privacy but often incur 
information loss and reduce data utility. Differential privacy, while practical, introduces 
noise that may compromise the accuracy of the ML models. In contrast, combining HE 
with logistic regression Zhou [23], Wei [24], Duchi [2], Machanavajjhala [1] facilitates 
privacy-preserving computations without sacrificing data utility or accuracy. Gupta, R. 
et  al. [30] Introduces a quantum machine learning-based model to predict malicious 
users in cloud networks, leveraging quantum algorithms to enhance anomaly detec -
tion and bolster network security. Gupta, R. et al. [31], Proposes a framework for secur -
ing IoT systems integrated with cloud services, addressing data privacy and integrity 
challenges through innovative security mechanisms. Later in the same year Gupta, R. 
et al. [32], Develops a privacy-preserving model for medical data using differential pri -
vacy and tri phase adaptive learning, ensuring data protection while maintaining utility 
and accuracy. In addition to it, Gupta, R. et al. [33], Presents a machine learning model 
incorporating differential privacy for secure data classification in cloud environments, 
suitable for privacy-sensitive applications. Gupta, R. et al. [34], Introduces a differential 
privacy-based model for handling sensitive data in multimedia and application-driven 
contexts, ensuring confidentiality without sacrificing usability.
Given the drawbacks of differential privacy and traditional methods, a framework that 
integrates HE with logistic regression is essential. The CKKS scheme, a variant of HE 
tailored for real-number operations, supports batch processing and efficient handling 
of large datasets. This makes it well-suited for constructing logistic regression models 
using encrypted healthcare data, ensuring that patient privacy is maintained throughout 
the process.
The motivation for this research is to address the pressing need for privacy-preserv -
ing heart disease prediction systems that balance robust data security with predictive 
accuracy. By leveraging FHE, the proposed approach ensures that patient data remains 
secure throughout the computational process, fostering trust in predictive analytics 
systems while adhering to stringent privacy regulations. This study aims to advance the 
future of secure healthcare analytics by demonstrating the feasibility and effectiveness 
of a fully homomorphic encryption-driven logistic regression model for heart disease 
prediction.
In healthcare, HE holds promise despite its limited application in practical ML tasks, 
particularly in integration with logistic regression (Wibowo [19], Yu [20]) models. Logis-
tic regression is a widely adopted ML method for classification, owing to its simplicity 
and competitive prediction accuracy. It is a foundational element in more complex clas -
sification algorithms such as gradient boosting. Logistic regression is a relatively light -
weight algorithm suitable for execution on mobile devices with limited computational 
resources. Mobile devices can participate in federated learning by locally training logistic

Logistic regression
Logistic regression is a statistical technique used to predict the likelihood of a binary 
outcome based on one or more explanatory variables. This method is frequently used 
in classification problems, where the objective is to estimate the probability of an 
instance belonging to a specific group.
The core principle of logistic regression involves examining the relationship 
between independent variables (also known as predictors or features) and the prob -
ability of an outcome falling into a particular category. Unlike linear regression, which 
forecasts continuous outcomes, logistic regression uses a logistic function to model 
the probability of binary results. The mathematical representation of the logistic 
regression model can be expressed
where:
• P (Y = 1|X) is the probability that the outcome variable Y equals 1, given the input 
feature X.
• e is the base natural logarithm.
• β1, β 2, β 3,.βn are the coefficients (parameters) associated with each independent 
variable  X1, X2, X3,….., Xn respectively.
• X1, X2, X3,…., Xn are independent variables.
The logistic function, known as the sigmoid function, transforms a linear combi -
nation of independent variables and coefficients into a value between 0 and 1. This 
ensures that the logistic regression output is a probability representing the likelihood 
of the observation belonging to the positive class (often coded as 1).
During training, the logistic regression model learns the optimal values for the coef -
ficients by maximizing the likelihood of observing the actual outcome given the input 
features. This process is typically achieved using optimization algorithms, such as 
maximum likelihood estimation or gradient descent.
Once the model is trained, it can predict the probability of an observation belong -
ing to a positive class based on its features. A common approach is to set a threshold 
(e.g., 0.5) such that observations with predicted probabilities above the threshold are 
classified as belonging to the positive class. By contrast, those below the threshold 
belong to the negative class.
Cheon‑Kim‑Kim‑Song
CKKS (Cheon‑Kim‑Kim‑Song) homomorphic encryption scheme
The CKKS (Cheon-Kim-Kim-Song) encryption scheme, introduced in 2016 by Kim, 
Cheon, Kim, and Song, is a ground breaking framework tailored for homomorphic 
encryption. This revolutionary technique enables computations of encrypted data 
without prior decryption, playing a pivotal role in scenarios such as cloud computing, 
where safeguarding data privacy throughout computations is paramount.

Homomorphic properties
CKKS was meticulously designed to support homomorphic addition and multiplica -
tion operations on encrypted data. This capability facilitates the direct execution of 
intricate mathematical operations on ciphertexts, ultimately producing results identi -
cal to those derived from decrypted plaintexts.
Efficiency
CKKS is strategically engineered for efficiency in homomorphic encryption, which 
generally entails higher computational demands than traditional methods. CKKS 
strives to optimize the performance of real-world applications, ensuring that the 
computational overhead is manageable.
Support for approximate arithmetic
Unlike certain homomorphic encryption schemes that strictly adhere to exact arith -
metic, CKKS proficiently handles approximate arithmetic. This flexibility is invaluable 
in applications, such as machine learning, where absolute precision in computations 
may not be imperative.
Components of CKKS
Parameter generation
CKKS requires the generation of specific parameters tailored to the security require -
ments and desired computational precision. These parameters intricately define 
the encryption scheme and the permissible range of achievable computations. Key 
parameters include:
- The polynomial modulus degree (n) defines the degree of the polynomial ring used.
- Coefficient modulus ( q) a large integer modulus determining the noise budget.
- Scaling factor ( /Delta1) a constant that scales plaintext values to ensure precision.
Key generation
The CKKS involves the generation of both public and private keys.
- Public key ( pk ) is used to encrypt data.
- Private key ( sk ) is used to decrypt data.
- Evaluation key ( evk ): Facilitates homomorphic operations on ciphertexts.

representation that is conducive to homomorphic operations. This process includes:

Proposed model
The proposed scheme is based on CKKS Fully Homomorphic Encryption and Logistic 
Regression over encrypted data. It offers an efficient methodology to support privacy 
at all levels, including the input, output, and model privacy. Whenever a patient visits 
a hospital based on hospital requirements, a patient securely shares his/her health 
information by encrypting health records using the hospital’s public key, which is con -
sidered a test dataset for heart disease prediction. The hospital uses CSP services to 
confirm whether the patient has heart disease. Initially, the hospital shares the train -
ing dataset and model parameters with the CSP in an encrypted form. CSP uses both 
the training dataset and model parameters to train the model. A gradient-based logis -
tic regression technique is used in the proposed method. While training the model, 
the CSP updates the weight vector accordingly to reduce loss or achieve more accu -
racy. The CSP shares the updated weight with the hospital. Subsequently, the hospital 
shares the test dataset with the CSP . The CSP uses the existing trained model against 
the test dataset and then returns the target value to the hospital in cipher form. Later, 
the hospital decrypts the received prediction results using their private keys. Subse -
quently, the hospital communicates it to the patient in cipher form by encrypting the 
target value using the patient’s public key. Finally, the patient uses their private key 
to decrypt and knows about his health status of does he has a heart problem or not.

Key concepts and polynomials in CKKS
i. Polynomial Ring R and Cyclotomic Polynomial:
- Let N be the degree of the polynomial, chosen as a power of 2.
- Define M = 2N , and the cyclotomic polynomial ϕ M (X) = XN+1.
- The polynomial ring R is defined as Z[X]/φM(X) , which means that polynomials in 
this ring are considered modulo XN + 1.
- The quotient ring Rq is Zq[X]/(XN + 1) , where operations are performed modulo 
both q (a large prime) and XN + 1.
ii. Canonical Embedding Function γ and Mapping π:
- The canonical embedding function γ maps elements from R to a subset of a higher-
dimensional space H.
- The mapping π :H → CN/2 transforms elements from the higher-dimensional space 
H to the complex vector space CN/2.
iii. Message Polynomial and Encoding.
- A message z in CKKS is initially represented as a vector in CN/2.
- T o encode this message into a polynomial format suitable for encryption, the following 
steps are performed:
- The message vector z ∈ CN/2 is first expanded to H by applying π−1(z).—It is then 
multiplied by a scaling factor /Delta1 and rounded to the nearest integer to form r.
- The polynomial m is obtained by applying γ−1(r) , resulting in the message in polyno-
mial format.

Security analysis
This section introduces mathematical frameworks for various privacy-related attacks on 
logistic regression and suggests homomorphic encryption (HE)-based defenses against 
these threats. We begin by describing the logistic regression model and examining potential

Results and interpretation
1. Computation Time Encryption time increased with higher polynomial sizes and 
epoch counts. The HELR model using 4096 was computationally more efficient than 
8192, making it suitable for resource-limited environments. However, 8192 offers 
stronger security, which may justify its higher overhead in critical applications.
2. Accuracy HELR models achieved an accuracy of 84%, closely matching the baseline 
Logistic Regression (LR) model’s 85%. This demonstrates that the homomorphic 
encryption process does not significantly compromise predictive accuracy, maintain-
ing its utility in privacy-preserving scenarios.
3. Security ROC analysis revealed that HELR_4096 had a higher AUC than HELR_8192, 
indicating better performance under certain configurations. This suggests that 
smaller polynomials may balance security and efficiency effectively in specific con -
texts.
4. Comparative Performance Across datasets, HELR consistently outperformed 
HESVM, particularly in the statlog dataset, where it achieved perfect scores. The 
results demonstrate HELR’s robustness and suitability for diverse data types and 
parameter configurations.
Interpretation
The findings confirm that the HELR framework offers a practical balance between 
privacy, computational efficiency, and predictive accuracy. While higher polynomial 
sizes enhance security, the trade-off in computational time should guide configura -
tion choices based on application needs. HELR’s superior performance across data -
sets underscores its potential for secure and effective healthcare predictions.

Conclusion and future work
In this study, we proposed an HE-aware logistic regression model built on encrypted 
data using CKKS. The proposed method can be used to outsource the training phase 
of logistic regression to a cloud service in a privacy-preserving manner. We conducted 
experiments using the Tenseal package on real datasets from Kaggle to evaluate the 
performance of both the logistic regression (over unencrypted data) and the proposed 
system. We conducted a security analysis to show that the proposed system can defend 
against poison, evasion, member inference, model inversion, and model extraction in 
the respective stages of machine learning. The performance of the proposed system pro-
vided satisfactory results relative to the model without HE, with a minimal difference 
in accuracy between 0.01 and 0.03. As a future scope, one can investigate and explore 
extension HELR framework to multi-class classification problems and propose HE-
aware multi class healthcare prediction systems.
